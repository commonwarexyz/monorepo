# Multimmit properties and evidence

This document maps implementation obligations to the
[Multimmit v4 specification](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21021v4). [`STATE_MACHINE.md`](STATE_MACHINE.md)
defines the corresponding ownership and transition boundaries. A property is covered when its owner,
legal mutations, durable witness, and appropriate evidence are all identified.

Evidence is classified as follows:

- **Executable test:** a finite, deterministic scenario or matrix that executes the named production
  mechanism. It is regression evidence, not a proof of a universal protocol claim.
- **Fuzz campaign:** a bounded deterministic interpreter or runtime scenario whose bytes select
  protocol actions, topology, failures, or structured artifacts. Saved inputs reproduce failures;
  mutation expands schedule coverage but does not turn the campaign into a proof.
- **Static/code inspection:** a type, owner, call graph, or absence claim checked against the current
  tree. This covers boundaries such as "consensus has no dense delivery cursor," "Catalog is the
  sole archive owner," or "certificates never enter `Automaton`."
- **Environment/liveness assumption:** a condition supplied by the protocol model or the attached
  application/runtime, not something an in-process test can establish. Tests may exercise a schedule
  satisfying the condition but cannot prove that the environment eventually supplies it.

Liveness assumes at most `f` faulty, crashed, or unavailable participants in total, eventual
post-GST message delivery, continuing runtime and storage service, and eventual application custody
for blocks from correct producers. Executions outside those assumptions must remain bounded and
fail closed, but need not progress.

## Marshal ordering and delivery

Marshal owns authenticated history acquisition, complete transaction-block retrieval, a deterministic
`Ord`/`Emit` ordering variant permitted by the specification, settled/unsettled sweeps, exact-block duplicate
reconciliation, and its durable delivery cursor. Application bodies remain opaque, so
transaction-level duplicate semantics remain application-owned. Consensus and marshal meet only at
the best-effort authenticated activity stream described by P-MATTACH-1.

## Specification v4 crosswalk

The status column distinguishes direct protocol mappings from explicit v4 optimizations, local
implementation refinements, and assumptions supplied by the environment.

| Specification item | Implementation properties | Mapping status |
|---|---|---|
| Model and cryptographic assumptions | P-ADM-1, P-SIGN-1, P-ASYNC-1 | Direct. Profiles require `n >= 5f + 1`; verification binds committee identity, context, and signed subject. Liveness additionally assumes at most `f` faulty or unavailable participants. |
| Section 4.1 transaction blocks and Algorithm 1 | P-AUTO-1, P-PIPE-1, P-DA-1, P-LIVE-1 | Direct, with an application fence. Headers bind chain, height, parent, and payload commitment. Core enforces one DA vote per height, contiguous ancestry, the depth-`d` window, and `n - 2f` certification. `Automaton::verify` adds local validity and custody before authority. |
| Section 4.2 leader blocks, proposals, and Algorithm 2 | P-COMMIT-1, P-PIPE-1, P-SIGN-1, P-VIEW-1, P-VQC-1 | Direct. The scheduled leader anchors on a greatest-view V-QC, proposes at most `d` blocks per chain, and receivers validate the referenced V-QC and nullification gap. Vote, novote, and nullify choices occupy durable exclusive slots. |
| Section 4.2 V-QCs and L-QCs | P-VQC-1, P-LEAD-1 | Direct. A V-QC contains complete, disjoint, attributed view messages and at least `2f + 1` votes for its designated proposal. An L-QC or retained pool has at least `n - f` distinct votes. |
| Section 4.2 `Tips` and `Tips*` | P-DA-1, P-TIPS-1, P-LEAD-1 | Direct for rank and carry thresholds. Safe tips use rank `f + 1` and support `2f + 1`; finalized tips use rank `3f + 1` and support `n - f` above a fully supported proposal. |
