## Statement
When the replica receives a notarization, nullification or finalization certificate from a
peer, the replica shall not accept it unless it attests votes of that kind, for that view (and
proposal, if any), from at least a quorum of distinct participants.

## Rationale
Issue #2070 shows a quorum reached by counting one signer twice. INV-0002 and INV-0003 cover
the certificates the replica assembles from votes. A Byzantine peer can skip assembly and send
a finished certificate directly, through the certificate broadcast or as a resolver response.
If the replica accepted a certificate whose signer set is below quorum, or where one
participant stands in for several, one faulty participant could fake support that honest
participants never gave. The replica could then enter a view, notarize a parent or finalize a
block without real quorum intersection. The same distinct-signer quorum rule must hold on every
path where the replica accepts a certificate, not only where it builds one.

## Evidence
Issue #2070 ("panic if there are several votes from one signer"): in a Twins test, two
validators signed with the same key, and another replica collected notarize votes
[signer 3, signer 3, signer 4] for view 5. With quorum 3 it counted three votes and started
assembly, although only two distinct participants had voted. Maintainers confirmed that
consensus must not count or pass duplicate signers ("we skip duplicates in `batcher`",
https://github.com/commonwarexyz/monorepo/issues/2070#issuecomment-3473238479). The issue was
closed by PR #2102 (merge commit 6ca07aba2fee6a37c166885abc94724c77f0b679). This invariant
extends the same quorum rule to certificates the replica receives from peers.

## Preconditions / assumptions
"Accept" means using the certificate for any protocol effect: entering a later view, adding it
to the replica's view state or journal, finalizing, answering or forwarding it, or reporting
it. Quorum is the committee quorum for the certificate's epoch. With attributable schemes
(ed25519, secp256r1, BLS multisig), the check applies to the certificate's signer set. With a
BLS threshold scheme, a recovered signature that verifies under the group key counts as proof
of a quorum of distinct shares.

## Observation hints
Certificates enter through the batcher's certificate channel (`actors/batcher/actor.rs`), the
resolver's fetch responses (`actors/resolver/actor.rs`) and the voter (`actors/voter`).
Verification goes through the scheme's `verify_certificate(s)`. For ed25519 it checks
`signers.count() >= quorum` in `cryptography/src/ed25519/certificate/mod.rs:240`, and the
`Signers` bitmap rejects duplicate indices (`cryptography/src/certificate.rs:709`). An
assertion can decode the signer bitmap of each accepted certificate and compare its count with
`participants().quorum()`.
